Le Palais Idéal du Facteur Cheval

About 1h15 from Les Cabritas, in Hauterives in the north of the Drôme, stands one of the most astonishing works in France: a dream palace built by hand, stone by stone, by an ordinary rural postman over 33 years.

The story of a dream come true

In April 1879, Ferdinand Cheval, a 43-year-old rural postman, stumbled on a stone so strangely shaped that it stirred an old dream within him. Self-taught, he then spent 33 years of his life, his rounds and his nights, building a palace in his garden inspired by nature and by the postcards and illustrated magazines he delivered along his route. He himself carved the tally of this achievement on the north façade: "10,000 days, 93,000 hours, 33 years of hardship."

A work of naive art hailed by the greats

From 1 January 1905, Ferdinand Cheval opened his palace to the public himself, setting up a guestbook and ticket office. His fame soon spread beyond the Drôme: as early as 1907, visitors from abroad were making the journey to Hauterives. It wasn't until the 1930s that artists such as Pablo Picasso, André Breton and Max Ernst posthumously hailed the genius of this self-taught man, who became a landmark figure of naive art. Le Palais Idéal was listed as a historic monument in 1969, a rare distinction for a work of outsider art.

Good to know before you visit

Allow a good hour to explore the palace's sculpted façades, turrets and interior galleries, as well as the mausoleum Cheval later built in the village cemetery. A museum space rounds out the visit, explaining how this unique work came to be. There is an entry fee, with a reduced rate for children.
