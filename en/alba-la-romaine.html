Alba-la-Romaine, Ancient Gallo-Roman Capital of the Helvii

About 42 minutes' drive from Les Cabritas, Alba-la-Romaine sits on the site of Alba Helviorum, capital of the Gallic Helvii people, founded around the turn of the Common Era. The present-day village, built in part over the ancient remains, stands out for its houses alternating ochre limestone and black volcanic basalt, a legacy of the lava flows that shaped the surrounding plateau. A Roman theatre, a medieval château and an archaeology museum make it a stop as rich in Roman history as in Provençal charm.

Alba Helviorum, a forgotten Roman city rediscovered

Founded as the administrative capital of the Helvii, Alba Helviorum acquired within a few decades a forum, baths, temples and a Roman theatre that could seat around 3,000 spectators — a striking sign of this provincial city's urban ambition. The town gradually declined from the 3rd century onward, likely weakened by invasions and the crisis of the Roman Empire, before being slowly abandoned. Archaeological digs carried out over several decades keep bringing this rich past to light, now presented at the MuséAl museum.

A charming village of ochre and black stone

Around the château, whose earliest structures date to the 11th century and which was reworked up until the Renaissance, the medieval village grew up using its own local materials: ochre limestone and black basalt from the plateau's ancient volcanoes. This colour contrast, unique in the Ardèche, gives the village's façades an instantly recognisable character, best discovered by wandering the lanes before or after visiting the ancient site.

Good to know before your visit

Allow about 42 minutes' drive from Les Cabritas Sud-Ardèche to reach Alba-la-Romaine. The archaeological site and the MuséAl museum can be visited separately or on a combined ticket depending on the season — check opening times before you go, as some parts close in the low season. The village is also lovely for a simple free-roaming stroll, with several spots offering views of the ancient theatre's remains right from the street.

Practical information from Les Cabritas

  • Best time to visit: spring and summer for the archaeological site's extended opening hours
  • Ideal for: Roman history enthusiasts, families, lovers of charming villages
